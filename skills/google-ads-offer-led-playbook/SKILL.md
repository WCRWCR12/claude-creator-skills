# Google Ads: offer-led playbook

## Honesty note
The source video is a cold-email offer framework, not a Google Ads tutorial. It says nothing about campaigns, keywords, bidding, budgets or tracking. This skill ports its offer-and-test method onto Google Ads. Tags: [VIDEO] from the video; [ADAPTED] translated for Google Ads; [STANDARD] normal Google Ads practice. Example business: a local plumber.

## The method in the video [VIDEO]
1. A front-end offer first: pitching the core offer cold converts poorly; a valuable lead magnet first, then the core offer, performs better (the presenter's numbers are illustrations, not data).
2. A good lead magnet takes real time and resources to deliver (a video of you solving their problem, a real guide). Buyers judge the change in state against their current pain, not a list of deliverables. AI-generated filler magnets do not work; use AI to find the angle, not to make the asset.
3. Research: use Claude as a market-research strategist. Given an industry and ideal customer, it builds search phrases to mine Reddit, forums and Hacker News for pain phrases. Redo regularly, as demand shifts.
4. Pain extraction: rank pains by how many separate people raise them (not one loud post), with intensity, verbatim quotes and hooks.
5. Offer: cluster signals into one offer per pain using the Hormozi value equation (dream outcome, perceived likelihood, time delay, effort/sacrifice).
6. Test, don't guess: run all 6 offers equally, rank by positive response rate, take the top 3 to round 2, scale the winner. Offers decay; retest.
7. Copy shapes: "I put together a quick video showing how I'd solve [problem], can I send it over?" and "Are you experiencing [problem]? I made a guide on solving it."

## Red flags in the claims
- Cold email is outbound; Google Ads is intent-based inbound. "1,000 sends per offer" must be translated to clicks.
- The conversion figures are hand-drawn illustrations, not data.
- The presenter sells an agency service: "you must have a front-end offer" is opinion.
- Reddit/forum complaint mining over-represents vocal, tech-leaning posters; a local tradesperson's customers may not be there. Cross-check with Search Console, Google Ads search terms, Facebook groups, review sites and five customer calls.
- Engagement counts are a popularity proxy, not demand.
- Scraping Reddit may breach its terms; search manually or use approved APIs.
- Guide/video lead magnets must genuinely be produced by the business. Do not fake expertise.

## Prerequisites (the owner provides)
- A Google Ads account, with access granted by invite. Never share or type passwords into chat.
- Billing set up by the account owner on Google's site. Never enter card details for someone else.
- Google Analytics 4 and Google Tag Manager access, or a developer who can add tags.
- Website admin access or a dev contact for landing pages; privacy policy and contact details live.
- Google Business Profile access for location assets and call tracking.
- A monthly budget ceiling and a maximum daily budget.

## Onboarding questions
1. What do you sell, average job value, gross margin?
2. Which service makes the most profit? What do you refuse to do?
3. What is a lead worth, and roughly how many leads become sales?
4. Service area, hours, can you answer calls? Missed-call plan?
5. Ideal customer, and bad fits?
6. Top 3 competitors and what they do better?
7. What do customers complain about before they call you?
8. Real proof: reviews, licences, years, named jobs, photos, guarantees you can honour.
9. Past Google Ads history, what happened, what spend?
10. Monthly budget ceiling and how fast do you need leads?
11. Where do leads go (phone, form, booking tool) and who follows up within 5 minutes?
12. Regulated industry (health, finance, legal, gambling, alcohol, licensed trades)?
13. What can you genuinely give away or demo as a front-end offer (audit, fixed-price quote, video walkthrough, checklist)?

## Runbook
**Phase 0, scope.** Run the onboarding questions. Work out break-even cost per lead from margin and conversion rate. If break-even cannot support realistic local click costs, say so before any build.

**Phase 1, offer research [VIDEO/ADAPTED].**
1. Define the ideal customer in one line (service + suburb + buyer type).
2. Mine pains from forums, Google autocomplete and People Also Ask, competitor 1-3 star reviews, Facebook groups and the business's own call notes. Record verbatim phrases.
3. Cluster. Keep only pains raised by many separate people. Rank the top 6.
4. Draft one offer per pain: free fixed-price quote, free site audit, 60-second video walkthrough, checklist, same-day callback. It must take real effort to deliver.
5. The owner approves the shortlist and any promise made.

**Phase 2, tracking first [STANDARD].** Nothing launches until conversions fire. GA4 + GTM, Google Ads conversion actions for calls, form submits and bookings. Enhanced conversions on. Test with real submissions. Link GA4, Google Ads, Search Console and Business Profile. Handle consent where required.

**Phase 3, build in draft, paused [STANDARD].**
- One Search campaign per service or intent group. Location targeting "presence", suburbs or radius set. Turn off Search Partners and Display expansion at the start.
- Keywords: tight ad groups per offer/intent. Start with phrase and exact; use broad only once there is conversion data (about 30+ conversions) and smart bidding. Favour high-intent terms ("emergency plumber [suburb]").
- Negatives from day one: jobs, careers, DIY, free, course, training, wrong areas, competitor names (unless deliberately conquesting). Review the search terms report.
- Bidding: Maximise Clicks with a CPC cap or manual CPC for the first 2-4 weeks, then Maximise Conversions, then target CPA only after about 30 conversions in 30 days.
- Budget: daily budget = target cost per lead x expected leads per day, never above the approved cap. Raise in small steps (about 20% max).
- Ads: Responsive Search Ads (up to 15 headlines, 4 descriptions), one ad group per tested offer, include location, honest price or proof, clear call to action. Run the claims check below.
- Assets: sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, call, location, price.
- Landing page: one per offer, message match to the ad, offer above the fold, phone + form, proof, fast on mobile.
- Small exact-match brand campaign to protect the name.
- Performance Max only after Search converts and tracking is solid.

**Phase 4, go/no-go.** Present a one-page launch pack: tracking evidence, structure, keywords, negatives, daily budget, monthly cap, ad copy, landing pages, claims check. The account owner gives an explicit yes, then enables it themselves on client-owned billing.

**Phase 5, test the offers [VIDEO/ADAPTED].** Up to 6 offers, each its own ad group with a similar budget share. Gate each round on roughly 300-500 clicks or 4 weeks, whichever first. Rank by cost per qualified lead, not click-through rate. Top 3 go to round 2, then the winner; pause the losers. Retest every 60-90 days or when conversion rate drops 30%.

**Phase 6, optimisation cadence [STANDARD].**
- Daily for the first 7 days: spend vs cap, disapprovals, tracking firing, search terms.
- Weekly: search terms (add negatives), device/hour/location, ad strength, pacing, call quality, lead follow-up speed.
- Fortnightly: offer and ad tests, landing page changes.
- Monthly: plain-English report (spend, leads, cost per lead, learnings, next test). Full account audit quarterly.
- Hands off bids during learning periods after big changes.

## Checklists
Pre-launch: conversion tracking tested; billing owned by the advertiser; budget cap documented; location settings verified; Search Partners off; negatives loaded; every ad claim evidenced; landing page live, mobile fast, privacy policy; policy-sensitive category checked; call answering plan; owner's yes recorded.
Weekly: spend within cap; no disapprovals; conversions firing; search terms reviewed; leads actually contacted; one change logged.

## Guardrails
- Claude drafts and paused builds only. Billing, spending, enabling campaigns and budget increases are the account owner's explicit decision.
- Hard monthly cap in writing, daily budget at or below it, pacing checked daily early on. Over cap: stop and ask.
- If you run ads for a client, the client pays Google directly; never front their ad spend.
- Consumer-protection law (misleading or deceptive conduct rules in your country): every claim ("best", "#1", "cheapest", "guaranteed", "free", "licensed", reviews, savings, before/after) needs evidence. Show total prices including taxes and mandatory fees. "Free" must be genuinely free. Testimonials must be real. Scarcity claims must be true. Check industry rules (trade licence numbers, health, finance, gambling, alcohol).
- Anti-spam and privacy law: consent and a privacy policy where the offer collects emails or phones; unsubscribe on follow-up; respect do-not-call registers.
- Google Ads policies: no misleading claims, no restricted-category content without certification, no trademark misuse, landing page must match the ad, follow editorial standards.
- Do not scrape sites against their terms. Research quotes are insight only, never reused as fake testimonials.
- Keep each client's data in that client's own folder.
