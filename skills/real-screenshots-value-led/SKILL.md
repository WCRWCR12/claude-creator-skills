# Real screenshots + value-led format

Technique inspired by: @create_daniel2 (Instagram)

This is the reference for what "real screenshots" means in a reel brief: not mocks, not AI-generated UI cards, not illustrated approximations. The actual interface and the actual data, as they exist in the product.

---

## What "real screenshots" means

Real screenshots are unmodified or lightly annotated captures of live software interfaces. They carry three properties mocks do not:

1. **Recognisability.** Viewers who use the tool see their own interface. Instant credibility.
2. **Specificity.** Real data, real labels, real button states. No generic placeholder copy.
3. **Trust signal.** The viewer's brain reads "this person actually uses this" before the voiceover says it.

AI-generated UI cards, illustrated dashboards and designed mockups signal inauthenticity. Even a high-fidelity mock reads as "I haven't done this myself."

---

## What "value-led" means

The hook and body are built around a specific, transferable outcome the viewer gets from watching.

- **Hook:** states the concrete value payload immediately. Not "here's something cool" but "here's the exact thing you can do or get in the next 60 seconds."
- **Body:** each segment delivers one piece of that value. Screenshots are the evidence layer, not the story. The voiceover is the story; the screenshot proves it is real.
- **Payoff:** the viewer leaves with something usable. The screenshot they saw is the thing they can now replicate.

Value-led is not the same as educational. It does not explain how something works. It shows the viewer a result they want, with real visual proof that it exists, fast enough that they cannot argue with it.

---

## Screenshot mechanics

### What to capture
- Real tool or app UI at the moment that proves the value claim (the specific output, setting or result)
- Actual numbers, copy and interface state, never stand-ins
- For a third-party product, capture it from your own account so it shows real usage

### What not to do
- Do not design a "screenshot-style" card. The difference is visible.
- Do not add heavy branded overlays that obscure the UI.
- Do not put a screenshot inside a Figma/Canva phone mockup. Use the screenshot directly.
- Do not round every corner and add drop shadows (the floating-card look reads as slop).
- No white outlines or faux-device frames unless the device is itself the subject.

### Frame proportion
- Screenshots usually fill a large share of the 9:16 frame (at least half the height).
- Stack two or three per segment if comparing or sequencing, but keep each readable.
- Captions run below or in a strip; the screenshot is not buried under text.

### Annotation style (when needed)
- Minimal highlights: a single colour underline or box.
- No decorative micro-text labels.
- If you circle or arrow something, make it hand-drawn in feel, not a software graphic.

---

## Reel shape

```
[HOOK: spoken + first screenshot]  opens on the value payload + the real visual proof
[SEGMENT 1]                        one step / tool / result + its screenshot
[SEGMENT 2]                        next step / result + screenshot
[...]                              repeat until the value is delivered (usually 3-5 segments)
[PAYOFF]                           synthesis: "that's how you get X"
[CTA]                              comment keyword / follow prompt: one word, no bundling
```

The screenshot appears at the same time as, or just after, the voiceover line that names it. Never show a screenshot before the voiceover has set up what the viewer is looking at.

---

## Application notes

- Use this as the benchmark whenever a brief says "real screenshots" or "show the actual tool".
- For reels that demo Claude: show the actual Claude interface, conversation and output, not a re-created card or an animated mock.
- For reels about real software (Notion, Figma, any SaaS): screenshot the live product at the moment that proves your claim.
- Motion graphics can frame, transition and annotate around screenshots, but the screenshot itself stays unmodified. The graphics are the wrapper; the screenshot is the proof.
- If you appear on camera, open (hook) and close (CTA) with your face and let screenshots carry the middle.

## What this is not

- Not a substitute for good scripting. The screenshot is the proof layer; the hook and voiceover structure still need a solid script (hook, amplify, examples, payoff, CTA).
