# Reel formats library

Each format was taken from a real reel, studied frame by frame, and written up as a remake template. Borrow the FORMAT only. Never reuse anyone's footage, music, characters or wording.

---

## 1. Fail, then product ("hook flip")
*Seen on: ColorCase (instagram.com/reel/DXPVLq9kRX9/), ~40k likes.*
- **0–5 s HOOK:** a raw phone clip where something happens (someone tumbles, a mess, chaos). No text, no link to the product.
- **5 s FLIP:** a hard cut, no transition. A one-word slam ("BAM!") works as the bridge.
- **5–12 s PAYOFF:** clean studio or macro product shots on a solid backdrop in the brand colour, about one shot per second: side, top, detail, low angle, with slow rotation or push-ins.
- **Length** 7–12 s. **Text:** none, or one word.
- **Brand remake:** an AI-generated or own-filmed mishap, then hero product shots in the brand colour.

## 2. Bait clip, casual line, inventory
*Seen on: a classic-car dealer (instagram.com/reel/DayRghmPl_v/), ~180k likes on a 7.8 s video.*
- **0–3 s:** an unrelated funny or scroll-stopping clip in one continuous shot (someone about to fall in water).
- **3 s:** a throwaway spoken bridge in a real voice: "See what everybody's up to? Let's go check it out."
- **3–8 s:** a hard cut to beauty shots of the product on a turntable or in a pan.
- **Length** 7–9 s and loopable. No on-screen text.
- **Use it for:** fast, high-volume filler between bigger pieces.

## 3. Fake-out transition
*Seen on: a streetwear brand (instagram.com/reel/DdvIwF6BR1z/).*
- **0–2 s:** a top banner promising a payoff, "Greatest transition ever 💀😂" or "Wait for it", over a hypnotic, satisfying loop.
- **2–14 s:** one continuous satisfying visual with no cuts and no voiceover. The banner stays up the whole time.
- **14–17 s:** a hard cut to the product, with a drop-date sticker or offer.
- **17–20 s:** one line of CTA text.
- **Length** 18–22 s. The joke is that the "transition" is just a cut to an ad.

## 4. Absurd "did you know" fact
*Seen on: a drum teacher (instagram.com/reel/DeNe4ReTFNG/).*
- **0–5 s HOOK:** an absurd claim stated deadpan as fact, with a matching visual (AI or 3D render).
- **5–14 s ESCALATION:** a new beat every 2–3 s.
- **14–17 s PIVOT:** the punchline lands on the brand (typing the URL, the product appearing).
- **17–24 s PAYOFF:** real footage of what's being sold.
- **24–32 s CTA:** one URL or action, said twice.
- **Captions:** one word at a time, bold white with a dark outline, bottom centre, synced to the voice (karaoke style). The voice is a flat, serious narrator.
- **Rule:** the story is obviously fiction. Never present a fake fact as real news.

## 5. Absurd micro-story with AI visuals
*Seen on: a used-car dealer (instagram.com/reel/DeWt7P2RHLh/).*
- **Structure:** a one-sentence absurd premise, then escalation ("but it actually worked"), a ridiculous payoff, the twist into the brand solving the real problem, and a one-line CTA.
- **Shots and pacing:** about 14 shots at ~2 s each, with fast zooms, whip pans and motion-blur transitions.
- **Visuals:** the first half is AI or CGI fantasy; the second half is real footage of the business or product.
- **Captions:** word by word in the bottom third, white sans, with a black box highlight on the key words.
- **Length** 25–30 s.

## 6. Dark-persona call-out
*Seen on: a sourcing agent (instagram.com/reel/DbUxDM8h1I3/).*
- **The hook is an audience call-out:** "This is a message to anyone who…". Then who I am and what I do in one line, then a bold, villain-trailer promise ("I'm coming for all of you…").
- **Edit:**
  - about 20 cuts in 20 s
  - dark, centred A-roll of the presenter, alternating with bright, blown-out SILHOUETTE B-roll (arms crossed, turning to camera), so every cut has a light/dark contrast
  - a tight crop on an eye or earring, a split 3-row stacked crop around 70%, and a slow push-in to finish
  - cuts land on words, and the rhythm speeds up in the last third
- **Text:** ONE keyword at a time (not every word), centred upper third, all caps, bold serif (Trajan or Cinzel style) in a strong colour with a soft shadow.
- **CTA:** none spoken; it's in the caption or bio.

## 7. Cartoon explainer funnel
*Seen on: a marketing explainer (instagram.com/reel/DeNNHePhomK/), 112 s.*
- **Style:** a two-character dialogue (expert plus clueless client) on a flat cream background with thick black outlines and ONE accent colour. A progress tracker ("LEVEL 1 2 3 4 5") fills as it goes, and UI mock cards (post, inbox, DM) pop in on the spoken word.
- **Pacing:** a new scene card every 2–4 s. A caption pill sits at the bottom with the keyword highlighted word by word.
- **Callbacks:** an early gag ("4 likes: mum and 3 bots") comes back as the punchline. A meta joke, "this video is itself the funnel", leads into a comment-keyword CTA.
- **Rule:** use your own original characters, never TV characters or cloned voices.

## 8. Talking head with motion-graphics inset
*Seen on: a motion-design tutorial (instagram.com/reel/DeZRVmkvhiR/).*
- **Layout:** the talking head fills the bottom half. Burned-in serif captions run 2 lines with a gold highlight on key words.
- **The top-half inset** cuts every 2–3 s to a different coded motion graphic (UI cards, screens). See the motion-graphics-render-loop skill.

## Choosing a format
- **Product launch or drop:** 1, 3 or 2.
- **Personality or founder-led brand:** 6 or 8.
- **Education or "why us":** 7 or 4.
- **Comedy and reach:** 5 or 4.
- **High-volume weekly filler:** 2.

## Always
- Hook in the first second; something happens in frame 1.
- Captions readable with the sound off. Keep inside the safe zones: 250 px clear at the bottom, 120 px at the sides.
- A new visual every 1–3 s unless the format relies on one long shot (3).
- Check the hook with hook-virality-check, and study competitors cheaply with video-contact-sheet.
- Licensed music only.
