# Lessons (read first, newest at the top)

Seeded from real review sessions. Add one dated line whenever a human catches something a cold watch missed.

- **Too strict is as useless as too soft.** Two reels a watcher marked FIX FIRST were called "perfect" by the reviewer and posted. FIX FIRST is only for things a normal viewer would notice and be put off by: cannot see it, cannot follow it, wrong picture for the words, a visible glitch, a word that sounds wrong. Taste goes under "could be better".
- **Do not judge motion from stills.** A watcher twice called a moving vehicle "parked" from half-second stills. Check consecutive frames or the video; if unsure, say "couldn't tell".
- **Readability fixes can make a shot worse.** Pushing for clarity produced pale, flat cut-away tunnels that looked like a cheap 2D graphic; the reviewer preferred the moody version and only wanted it brighter. Feel outranks tidy: ask for more light, never a diagram.
- **Flat top-down diagrams fail** even when clear ("looks like clip art", "a school diagram"). A top-down view must still look like the 3D world.
- **Clipped line endings were missed by every cold pass** (a line lost the D of "band", up to four times in one reel). Do a sound-only listen to each line END, and read the sound check's line-endings row.
- **Wrong or missing machine parts and a column of vehicles parked nose-to-tail** were missed because the watcher only read phone-size stills. For any machine or vehicle shot, also look at full-resolution crops against a real reference photo, and read the staging of every multi-vehicle shot.
- **Things reviewers spot in seconds**: barbed wire missing on a "wire" shot, a vehicle that looks like it is hovering, a turret that should be turning, water not sitting in its dip, figures that look lifeless.
- **Dark scenes**: "the lighting is cool but I can't see what's happening". At phone size, can you trace the outline of the thing that matters?
- **Builds that look like a scrap heap**: when something is assembled, can you see the order it goes together in?
- **Who am I now?** A script that switched the viewer between sides ("you're Athens... you're Rome") lost the viewer. One side, one colour, the whole reel.
- **A label using a word the voice never says**, or a picture that shows the opposite of its line, or a shot that gives away the next shot's reveal: all real misses.
