# Mechanism reveal

Intellectual suspense: the viewer cannot answer the question but wants to, and the 3D answer starts unfolding within a second or two.

## Hook
`How does <object> <unusual verb> <unexpected constraint>?` The surprise sits in the constraint: "without fingers", "without digging", "uphill", "through its own propeller". No preamble. Frame 1 already shows the object moving.

## Body: 4-6 mechanism beats, 5-8 s each
Each beat:
1. State the sub-problem ("The crop is tangled at the stem.").
2. Show the part that solves it, named plainly ("A spinning disc cuts it clean.").
3. The part locks in or runs; a short label appears in the lower third.
4. Next sub-problem starts straight away.
The machine is the subject (no "you"). Sentences of 6-10 words. Camera tracks or orbits the part being named, close; then pulls back to show where it sits in the whole.

## Payoff
Visual: the full machine running, all parts working together, one slow orbit. A short closing line can name it and what it does.

## Direction
- Camera always moving: orbit, track, push in on the part, pull back to context (skill `scene-3d`).
- Parts assemble in the order a builder would place them: frame first, working parts last. Never a scrap heap of parts flying together.
- Cutaways: fade the outer shell to 15-25% opacity to show internals moving (skill `hardware-hero-motion`).
- Materials and colours of the real machine; clean, readable light.
- Labels: white, short, lower third, real names only.
- Accuracy matters: engineers and farmers will check. Use real references.

## When to use something else
If people and their decisions drive the story, use `build-up-script` (the "you" loop). If the physics of one surprising event is the story, use `myth-explainer`.
