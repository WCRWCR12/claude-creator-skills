# The shots

You own the picture for every word. Send back any line that cannot be shown in the time it takes to say.

## The one rule
**One clause, one shot, and the shot shows exactly those words.** When the narrator says "off a table" the frame is the table edge. Work down the caption chunks: every two or three chunks is a cut, and every cut lands on the start of a chunk.

## Shot budget
- Part A: 17-21 shots in 18-22 s. Most are 0.8-1.3 s. Nothing under 0.6 s. One long shot (2-3 s) is allowed, and it is the map.
- Part C: 4-6 shots in 5-8 s.
- The man's face is in 40% of the part A shots or fewer.
- The camera never sits still: a slow push, a drift, or a move with the object. Small moves, about 3-6% of the frame over a one-second shot.

## The shot cycle (use it in this order, then vary)
| Beat | Shots, in order |
|---|---|
| The dare | 1. Side medium, the man already mid-act at the frame edge, the main prop a line across the frame. 2. Close on his hand as the act happens (inside the first 0.6 s). 3. Travel with the object. 4. Close on where it is going. 5. A detail insert (what the hand or tool does after). 6. The far end of the set-up, facing the lens |
| The wrong guess | 7. **The map**: straight down from above on the whole set-up, the man small. Real 3D, never a flat diagram. 8. Low three-quarter as the guess plays out |
| The denial | 9. Medium on the man, the consequence about to arrive. 10. The consequence, then its aftermath from above |
| The simpler case | 11. The new set-up: his hand at one edge, the new prop at the other. 12-13. The new prop grows until it fills the frame. 14. Push in on the object in flight. 15. Macro on the moment the principle shows, camera drifting round it |
| The cut | 16. Back in the open room, the first test again, tighter. 17. Macro at the point of no return, shallow focus. 18. A see-through cutaway or split view of the hidden moment. Cut on the noun |
| The answer | 19. The thing happens, in one clear side view. 20. The why, shown (a level line, a wide arc, two things side by side). 21. The result at ground level. 22. The simpler-case props again, doing the same thing: the callback |

Rules inside the cycle:
- **Frame 1 is already moving.** The act happens by 0.6 s.
- **The second set-up must look like a new place** without leaving the room: let the new prop fill the frame so the background changes colour. The eye gets a reset at the halfway mark.
- **Small things are drawn big** (four to eighteen times life size, biggest in the map shot) and fast things move slowly enough to follow, with a thin bright trail. No "slow motion" label.
- **One clock.** When the object is slowed, the man is slowed with it. Drive both from the same variable.
- **The cutaway is the last picture before the cut.** It shows the moment just before the answer, never the answer.
- **The wrong guess is acted out for real**, dry and bloodless.
- **Compute the physics.** Fall time is sqrt(2h/g); put the equation in the code and take the timing from it.

## Captions
The only text on screen. White heavy all-caps, dark outline, centred, centre near y 1285 of 1920 (allowed 1250-1320), cap height about 56 px, 1-3 words, changing on the spoken word. Keep that band clear in every frame: no prop, hand or bright line behind it.

## Dailies questions (ask of every shot)
1. Mute it: do I still know which words this shot belongs to?
2. Is anything in the caption band?
3. Is something moving in every frame?
4. If the man is in it: where did his motion come from, and would I believe a person moves like that?
5. Is this angle different from the last two?
