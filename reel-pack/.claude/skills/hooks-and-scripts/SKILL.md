# Hooks and scripts

## The hook (frame 1, first ~2 s)
The first spoken line and the first frame are the same idea, and something is already moving. No warm-up, no "hey guys", no context first. Pick one type:

| Type | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge gap question | How does X do Y (without Z)? | "How did a pilot shoot through his own propeller?" |
| Belief attack | The cynical thing the viewer already thinks, then a pivot | "Coded animation always looks cheap. This one isn't." |
| Myth test | What would really happen if...? | "What actually happens if you fire a bullet straight up?" |
| Number scaled up | One physical number, made huge | "This wall stopped 300,000 men." |
| Product line | What the thing does, said plainly | "This tool turns one sentence into a 3D animation." |
| Result first | The end state, then rewind | "This took nine minutes. Here's how." |

Test it: would the viewer nod, frown or wonder in under two seconds? Is it TRUE to what follows? A hook the video does not pay off kills trust.

## Structures
- **Value-led (tools, skills, products)**: HOOK, second hook (the one thing it does), OPEN LOOP (a reason to stay), VALUE x3 (one short concrete line each: a thing it does, not a claim), PAYOFF (the outcome in one line), CTA. Fast.
- **Mechanism reveal**: question hook, 4-6 beats where each beat states a sub-problem and shows the part that solves it, visual payoff (the whole thing working). See `mechanism-reveal`.
- **Build-up**: number hook, 5-7 rungs addressed to "you", each fix causing the next problem, the proper name said last. See `build-up-script`.
- **Myth test**: the question, the common guess, the test, the honest answer with its one caveat. See `myth-explainer` (five script moves, a sentence that breaks off on its noun, an answer with a short build-up).

## Writing rules
- **Plain words a 12-year-old gets.** Everyday comparisons ("as heavy as a car").
- **Full sentences.** Clipped fragments ("Arrows first. Then fire.") sound machine-written. So do stock phrases: "but here's the thing", "plot twist", "game-changer", "let's dive in".
- **One idea per line**, one line per shot. If a line cannot be SHOWN in the time it takes to say it, rewrite the line (the director will send it back anyway).
- **Every picture matches its line** at the moment it is said. Never show the product/payoff before it is named.
- **One "you"** for the whole reel; never swap who the viewer is.
- **A signature phrase once**, then vary the lead-ins ("so then you", "which leads to", "now") or drop them.
- **Facts checked** by the contextualiser before voicing. Numbers spelled as said ("nineteen forty-four").
- **Pace for voice**: about 150-165 words a minute; hooks can run a little faster, the payoff slowest. A 30 s reel is about 75-80 words.

## Pattern interrupt (optional, one per reel)
One deliberate rhythm break at 25-40% of the runtime or just before the payoff: a line flip, an audio drop (music out for 1-3 s while the voice carries on), a punch-in, or a tone shift. Still a full sentence that carries the story. Never in the first 3 s or on the CTA. No glitch transitions or flashing words.

## On-screen text
- 1-3 words of headline at most, in the lower third. Word-by-word captions (live word highlighted) in the lower third too, 62-72 px on a 1080-wide frame.
- Real content only: no tiny monospace labels, status strips, fake readouts, eyebrow tags.

## The CTA
One action. Comment-a-keyword ("Comment GUIDE and I'll send it") works when an auto-reply is actually set up; otherwise "follow for part 2" or "save this". Say it warmly, never salesy, and keep the tail short.

## Post caption
Line 1 is a second hook or a "save this if..." line. Then 2-4 short plain paragraphs or a short numbered list of what they will learn. End with the same single CTA. 1-3 relevant hashtags, not thirty.

## Output format for a script
```
HOOK (0-2 s): <line>                      | SHOT: <what we see>   | TEXT: <lower third or none>
BEAT 1 (2-8 s): <line>                     | SHOT: ...             | TEXT: ...
...
PAYOFF: <line>                             | SHOT: ...
CTA: <line>                                | SHOT: ...
Word count: N  (~N/2.6 s at 155 wpm)
```
