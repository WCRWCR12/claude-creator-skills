# Answer and source (never on screen): sideways motion does not change how fast a thing falls, so a ball thrown level and a
# ball dropped from the same height at the same instant land together (air drag makes a tiny difference). Any school
# physics text on projectile motion; fall time t = sqrt(2h/g).

A: If you threw a ball flat off a rooftop and dropped another at the same moment, most people assume the dropped
   one would land first. But that's not exactly what would happen. You see, if you rolled a marble off a table and
   let one fall beside it, they would hit the floor together. But when you throw one and drop one, the two balls

C: fall at exactly the same rate. The thrown one just travels a long way while it falls, so they land at the same
   moment, just like the marbles.
